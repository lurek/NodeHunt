Decentralized broadband is a broad idea: instead of one company owning every network layer, a protocol can coordinate independent operators, hardware, and incentives. That idea should be evaluated through real coverage, service reliability, legal compliance, and user protections—not a token narrative alone.

## Infrastructure comes first

Any broadband network depends on physical deployment, backhaul, spectrum or local access rights, hardware maintenance, and customer support. A credible project explains which pieces it owns, which it coordinates, and what operators are responsible for.

## Questions worth asking

Who provides connectivity? What data is collected? How are outages handled? Are hardware costs refundable? Is the service available and lawful where you live? Clear answers to those questions matter more than a promotional dashboard.

## Read incentives carefully

Rewards can be revised, delayed, or discontinued. If participation requires buying hardware or locking assets, model the downside first. Only use funds and devices you can afford to commit or lose.
